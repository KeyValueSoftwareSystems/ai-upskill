Beyond the Quencher: Inside Stanley 1913’s food and beverage expansion

Armed with global consumer research, athlete endorsements and rapid product drops, the 113-year-old brand is betting on everyday rituals.

Stanley 1913 has spent five years riding a growth wave around one product: the Quencher. First introduced in 2016, the 40-ounce tumbler first became a cultural phenomenon and, for many, an everyday accessory as ubiquitous as a smartphone —and a canvas for self-expression routinely paired with outfits and manicures.
Now, the 113-year-old brand is making its most ambitious move yet: evolving from a hydration hit-maker into a broader food and beverage lifestyle brand.
“We really see this moment as the start of a new chapter for Stanley,” said Kate Ridley, the company’s chief brand officer, who joined the brand after years at Allbirds and Adidas.
Ridley frames the expansion as a “regrounding.” Stanley built its legacy on food and beverage rituals — from early 20th-century industrial workers carrying hot coffee and soup through harsh shifts, to outdoors enthusiasts navigating rugged terrain, to modern wellness consumers using the Quencher.
“If I had a dollar for every person who told me their grandfather took them fishing and always had the green Stanley bottle…” she said.
Diversifying into café products, mobility and storage represents the next evolution of that legacy, grounded in an eight-month global study of more than 5,000 consumers across six countries, Ridley said. The research surfaced a key insight: the difference between a routine and a ritual.
“A routine really refers to something that you do time and time again, but a ritual is something that has a much deeper meaning for you, and you do it with intention,” Ridley said. “So the way that people make their matcha, or are absolutely obsessive about how they brew their coffee or meal prep for the week … This is where Stanley can have deep connection and meaning.”
Three pillars of growth
The strategy rests on three levers: product, consumer and geography.
Product diversification moves beyond the Quencher into café items such as the Transit Flip Top Mug and Café-To-Go Travel Mug, plus a new mobility and storage category. Geographically, the EMEA region leads Stanley’s growth, up 36% year-over-year behind strong European coffee and commuter culture, Ridley said.
On the consumer side, Stanley is building on its strong standing with young women to court men and parents — 60% of its target base has kids at home, opening potential categories such as back-to-school lunch gear.
The launches have paced at five new product lines in 10 weeks, a speed that Ridley credits to how closely the team works together: 50 global leaders across design, product, marketing and supply chain all work from the same consumer insights, so products and campaigns launch in lockstep.
Partnerships that feel earned, not bought
Stanley’s marketing muscle shows up most clearly in its partnerships, from Olivia Rodridgo to Post Malone, guided by a strict rule: only work with existing fans. Lionel Messi stands out as the prime example — the brand says the soccer player has used Stanley products since childhood and drinks mate, a traditional South American herbal tea, from a Stanley cup before every practice and match.
As part of the multi-year partnership, Messi-branded mate products sell out every time, Ridley said. The fourth Messi collaboration, the Stanley 1913 x Messi Gold Collection, launched in May as what the brand calls its most premium tier yet.
